# PTSD Coach: What the Literature Actually Says (2026-09-05)

The app feels nice to use. The literature says: modest efficacy at best,
catastrophic retention always. Both true at once — nice-to-use is the trap:
everyone downloads, uses it 3 times, leaves. Full brief from deep study;
DOIs for every claim.

## Trials (efficacy)
| Study | N | Result | Authors' own limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kuhn 2014 pilot | 45 vets, 3 days | 90% satisfied; helpfulness 68–91% | No control; efficacy untested |
| Miner 2016 pilot RCT | 49 | Within-group d=0.58; between-group n.s. (d=0.25) | "Efficacy inconclusive" |
| **Kuhn 2017 full RCT** (only full RCT) | 120 | Response 46.8% vs 25.9% waitlist BUT **no significant between-group mean difference** | "Much smaller than psychotherapies"; no dose-response |
| Possemato 2016 pilot | 20 | Both arms improved; supported arm 70% referral uptake | N=20, single site |
| Possemato 2023 multi-site | 234 | Clinician-rated severity: **NO difference**; small patient-reported edge (d=0.28) | "Not superior on primary outcomes" |
| **Bröcker 2023 meta (17 studies)** | — | Pooled effect vs control **NOT significant** (SMD −0.19, p=.09) | "Evidence on effectiveness remains limited" |
| Van Stolk-Cooke 2023 (Family) | 200 | ~50% never opened the app; no group differences | Extremely low engagement |

## Attrition (the killer numbers)
| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime use (avg) | **3 sessions / 18 min TOTAL** | Hallenbeck 2022 (207k downloads) |
| Day-2 retention | **8.5%** (from 87% day 1) | Hallenbeck 2022 |
| 1-year retention | **0.69%** | Hallenbeck 2022 |
| First-timers reaching content | **37%** | Owen 2015 (154k downloads) |
| Tech difficulties reported | **33%** (Android worse) | Owen 2015 |
| Usability (SUS) | **66.25 — low for mobile** | Rodriguez-Paras 2017 |

## Why nice ≠ retained (documented flaws)
Veteran/combat framing alienates civilians + CPTSD; 17 permissions
(Congress: "disturbing"); repeated PCL-5 as chore; same 21 tools for
everyone; robotic TTS; no personalization; no panic entry (menu maze
mid-panic); crisis-line spam over human bridge.

## What this means for Anchor (USP, honestly)
Symptom tools are commodities — correctly copied (breathing, grounding,
sleep tips, trackers). The USP was never the tools; it is:
1. **Delivery:** one-tap haptic-first entry vs menu maze (no panic button exists there).
2. **Routing:** dissociation-gated selection (no app does this).
3. **Learning:** outcome-tracked reranking (their tools are static).
4. **Access:** offline-first, zero-permission, India-ready (their 17-permission US-VA model).
5. **Voice:** custom safety phrase bound to the button.
Location awareness was garnish — correctly cut, no loss. "India-specific"
is not garnish: 80% treatment gap + zero localized PTSD apps in 1.4B
people is distribution no one else owns — but it is distribution, and the
product wedge above must stand on its own.
