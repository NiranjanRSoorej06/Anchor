# Stitch Prompt — Anchor app description + mood (copy-paste to Google Stitch)

Anchor is an offline-first Android app for people living with PTSD. It is a
pocket grounding companion: when distress hits, you open the app, tap one
giant ANCHOR NOW button, and the phone guides you down — slow vibration,
a breathing visual, a whispered calming voice in your earbuds — then asks
one question (Better, Same, or Worse), and either closes gently into
recovery or offers a discreet pre-written message to someone you trust.
Everything happens on the phone itself: no account, no internet needed,
nothing uploaded, nothing that says PTSD on the surface.

The core loop is: one tap → calming vibration + breathing guide + voice →
"how does it feel now?" → recovery screen or safety screen → optional
reach-out → a quiet closing screen that confirms everything stayed private.

The app has six areas: (1) a home screen that is almost entirely one big
calm button; (2) a session screen with a slow pulsing visual, a check-in
question, and recovery/safety endings; (3) a Tools library of guided
exercises (grounding scans, breathing patterns, muscle relaxation, sleep
tips, calming phrases); (4) a Find Support section with crisis helplines
(Indian numbers like Tele MANAS 14416), nearby clinics via map, therapist
directories, and peer communities; (5) a gentle onboarding that asks a few
optional questions, each skippable, to personalize the tools; (6) a safety
plan screen with the user's own warning signs, coping steps, and contacts.

It is built India-first for all trauma types (not just veterans): floods,
conflict, domestic violence, accidents, childhood trauma. Many users will
open it mid-panic with shaky hands and almost no attention to spare, often
at night, often needing nobody around them to know what the app is.

MOOD / FEELING: a safe harbor at 2am. Quiet confidence, not cheerfulness —
warm but never chirpy, clinical nowhere in sight. The feeling of someone
sitting beside you without demanding anything: steady, unhurried, private.
Dignity over delight; calm over color. It should feel like exhaling. A
harbor light, not a hospital corridor and not a candy-colored wellness app.
Discreet enough to open on a crowded train. Human enough that a whispered
voice saying "you are in a safe place" feels like it means it.
