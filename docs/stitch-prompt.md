# Stitch Prompt — Anchor, every feature built + planned (copy-paste to Google Stitch)

Anchor is an offline-first Android app for people living with PTSD. It is a
pocket grounding companion: when distress hits, you open the app (or hold a
volume button, or tap a home-screen widget), tap one giant ANCHOR NOW
button, and the phone guides you down — slow vibration, a breathing visual,
a whispered calming voice in your earbuds — then asks one question (Better,
Same, or Worse), and either closes gently into recovery, tries a different
exercise, or stops everything into a safety screen. It can then offer a
discreet pre-written message to someone you trust, and closes by confirming
everything stayed on the phone. No account, no internet needed, nothing
uploaded, nothing that says PTSD on the surface.

## Everything in the app (built + planned — design for all of it)

**Entry (built):** giant ANCHOR NOW button that fires a haptic before the
next screen loads; volume-button long-press trigger that works from the
lockscreen; home-screen widget that opens grounding directly.

**Grounding session (built):** slow heartbeat vibration and tactile
breathing haptics (4-second inhale swell, 6-second exhale fade) synced to
a pulsing breathing visual at a slow resonant pace; whispered voice saying
"you are in a safe place" routed privately to earbuds with silent
vibration-only fallback; static 5-sense grounding script screen; full
session loop (grounding → easing → check-in → recovery, retry, or safety
stop) with cancel anytime.

**Check-in + learning (built engine, screens coming):** one-tap Better /
Same / Worse after every session; the app learns which exercises help each
person and shows an insight card ("Breathing helped 4 of 5 times");
post-session gentle follow-up (trigger + distress + short note); episode
history.

**Exercises library (content built, browser coming):** guided grounding
scans, breathing patterns (box, coherent, long exhale), full muscle
relaxation, sleep hygiene checklist, coping statements, safety phrases,
two Indian-context visualizations (monsoon garden, quiet courtyard).

**Custom comfort (planned):** user-built calming routines bound to the
panic button; own voice recordings (a wife's voice telling you to calm
down) with bundled fallback phrases; natural warm voices in Hindi, Tamil,
Telugu and English.

**Find Support (built data, screen coming):** crisis helplines led by
Tele MANAS 14416 plus regional numbers (Sneha, Kashmir Lifeline, NIMHANS,
Vandrevala), pincode-typed nearby institution finder, map shortcuts,
therapist directories, peer communities (forums, listener services,
Indian NGOs) — ordered crisis-first with clear online/offline labels.

**Onboarding (spec'd, screen coming):** welcome → consent → one-time
symptom check-in (PCL-5 questionnaire, 20 items) → situation picker →
sensory preferences → safety phrase → trusted contact → done; every step
skippable, ~15 seconds bare path.

**Safety (built engine, screens coming):** dissociation-awarerouting that
excludes inward-focus exercises when dissociated; hard safety filter over
every suggestion; Stanley-Brown safety plan form; safety-stop screen that
does nothing automatic, ever.

**Reflection + follow-up (planned):** gentle post-session notification
leading to an optional 3-tap check-in (trigger + distress rating + short
note for the therapist); private episode journal with therapist export;
voluntary trigger-photo log (blurred thumbnails, never auto-reviewed).

**Look + system:** three calm color palettes (Nord default, Soft Sage,
Warm Sand) with light and dark modes; personalization (theme, routines,
phrases); progress graphs; Learn education module; multilingual UI;
in-app community (planned); everything stored on-device only.

## Who it's for
India-first, all trauma types, explicitly not just veterans: floods,
conflict, domestic violence, accidents, childhood trauma. Many users open
it mid-panic with shaky hands and almost no attention to spare, often at
night, often needing nobody around them to know what the app is.

## MOOD / FEELING
A safe harbor at 2am. Quiet confidence, not cheerfulness — warm but never
chirpy, clinical nowhere in sight. The feeling of someone sitting beside
you without demanding anything: steady, unhurried, private. Dignity over
delight; calm over color. It should feel like exhaling. A harbor light,
not a hospital corridor and not a candy-colored wellness app. Discreet
enough to open on a crowded train. Human enough that a whispered voice
saying "you are in a safe place" feels like it means it.
